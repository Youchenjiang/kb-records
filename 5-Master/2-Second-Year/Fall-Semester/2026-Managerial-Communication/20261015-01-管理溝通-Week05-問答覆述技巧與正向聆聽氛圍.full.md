## 🎯 問答環節弱勢回應置換與專業覆述策略 (Replacing Weak Responses)

**【授課講師】**：For this one, actually, the answer is already here on the slide. You can actually just read this one, it is also no problem. The key thing is that you just try to practice the wording by speaking it out loud.

**【授課講師】**：Let's look at the weak responses and how we can paraphrase them professionally. For example, have you ever heard someone say: *"I already explained this"*? Sometimes you might experience this kind of situation, right? You may see this kind of defensive response from someone who is presenting something: *"I already explained this!"*

**【授課講師】**：This kind of response is actually not respectful to your audience at all. It feels offensive or defensive, as if you are blaming the audience for not listening carefully. Instead of reacting defensively, we can respond much more professionally by stating something like:
> *"If I understand correctly, your concern is [restate concern]..."*

**【授課講師】**：By doing this, we paraphrase or re-state the question from the audience calmly and constructively.

**【授課講師】**：Let's look at another example. Another typical weak response is: *"That is not what I mean!"* This is also not a constructive response. Instead of saying it that way, which sounds dismissive, we can professionally respond like:
> *"Let me restate that in another way..."* or *"Let me clarify that point first."*

**【授課講師】**：If it feels too difficult to completely paraphrase on the spot, you can simply restate the core question as it is, just to make sure that you and the audience are on the exact same page.

**【授課講師】**：Another common weak response is: *"You misunderstood my point."* Again, this places the blame directly on the listener. Instead of stating it this way, we can reframe it positively:
> *"Are you asking whether the proposal is feasible under [specific constraint or condition]?"*

**【授課講師】**：Frame the response depending on your specific situation. This shows that you are actively listening, respect the audience's inquiry, and are ready to address the substance of their question rather than arguing about misunderstandings.

## ❓ 觀眾問題類型分類與應對策略 (Question Typology & Strategies)

**【授課講師】**：The next concept is about question types. This is closely related to the paraphrasing techniques we just discussed, because not all questions require the exact same response strategy.

**【授課講師】**：Broadly speaking, there are several distinct types of questions: **Open Questions**, **Closed Questions**, and **Directed or Challenging Questions**. As a presenter, you must be able to identify what kind of question is being asked so that you can appropriately formulate your answer.

**【授課講師】**：First, what is an **Open Question**? An example is: *"How will this proposal affect staff morale?"* This question is very broad and open-ended. If you try to answer everything, you might wander off-topic for ten minutes. In this case, we can politely narrow down the scope or re-ask: *"What specific aspect of staff workflow would you like me to address?"*

**【授課講師】**：Second, what is a **Closed Question**? A closed question asks for a specific fact, figure, or binary confirmation. For example: *"Does this proposal include the operational hardware budget?"* It is directly related to a concrete point or data item. For this type, you should directly confirm or clarify the factual details without excessive over-explanation.

**【授課講師】**：Third, what is a **Directed or Challenging Question**? An example is: *"Isn't this project far too risky for our timeline?"* This kind of question is often pointed, skeptical, or even interrupting before you have fully finished your explanation.

**【授課講師】**：Identifying the question type before choosing your response strategy is vital. By understanding the question structure and paraphrasing effectively, even when you face a challenging question that you do not immediately have a perfect answer for, you can still control the situation gracefully. At the very least, your calm composure will prevent unnecessary escalations or defensive arguments.

## 👂 營造正向聆聽氛圍與防衛心理調控 (Listening Climate)

**【授課講師】**：The next part is about cultivating a **Positive Listening Climate**. A positive climate makes questions much easier to ask and significantly easier to answer constructively.

**【授課講師】**：What does a positive listening climate require from the presenter? First, keep facial expressions neutral, open, and attentive—avoid rolling your eyes, frowning, or showing signs of impatience. Second, never interrupt before the question is complete. Let the questioner finish their entire sentence. Even if you already anticipate what they are going to ask, do not cut them off.

**【授課講師】**：Third, acknowledge the concern without over-promising or over-phrasing. Validate that their question is legitimate and worth addressing. And finally, treat disagreement as valuable information, not as a personal attack. Under pressure, presenters often feel personally targeted when someone challenges their slides. You must separate your personal ego from the project data. Regard disagreement as critical feedback that can help refine your project proposal.
