## 🌪️ Hans 發表：離岸風電預測、多模型交叉防護與技術轉譯挑戰

**【Hans】**：Good morning everyone. I'm Hans, a master's student in Information Management. Today I'm excited to share three key milestones from my academic and technical journey. My guiding principle has always been: "An AI system can only be as good as the empirical foundation upon which it is built."

**【Hans】**：My first milestone was my undergraduate capstone project: predicting offshore wind power generation along Taiwan's western coastline. Because wind patterns in the Taiwan Strait are notoriously erratic, simple time-series models failed. I engineered domain-specific meteorological features and built robust ensembles that outperformed standard baselines. My second achievement involved competitive machine learning, where our team ranked high on international scoreboards by refining feature pipelines.

**【Hans】**：My third and most recent project focuses on LLM security safeguards. Many enterprises are deploying AI agents, but they face severe risks of hallucinations and adversarial prompt injection. In our project, I implemented a cross-source comparison architecture as a safeguard: outputs are cross-verified across independent sources before being served to end-users. The hardest challenge was never the model code itself, but translating complex machine learning assumptions into terms that non-technical business clients could confidently defend and trust.

**【授課教師】**：Thank you so much, Hans! That was a very well-structured presentation. You articulated your core message clearly and maintained strong audience rapport. Now, let's open the floor for one question. Yes, from the floor, please go ahead.

**【提問學員】**：Hi Hans, which project among the three was the most technically challenging for you, and why?

**【Hans】**：Thank you for the question. The LLM red-teaming and prompt defense project was definitely the hardest. Because in adversarial security, you must think like an attacker. You are not merely optimizing a passive loss function; you are anticipating dynamic exploits, circumventions, and out-of-distribution inputs. It pushed me to deeply examine system boundaries.

## 💾 Victor 發表：跨部門三個月伺服器遷移與異地備份實務

**【Victor】**：Good morning, everyone. I'm Victor, specializing in cybersecurity and software engineering. Today I chose to share a project where managerial communication—not just coding—was the ultimate determining factor: a three-month enterprise server migration and offsite disaster recovery backup implementation.

**【Victor】**：On paper, the technical task seemed straightforward: migrate legacy enterprise database instances to new physical hardware, reconfigure network interfaces, and establish an automated offsite backup pipeline using the decommissioned hardware. However, the execution required relentless coordination between external clients, internal infrastructure teams, senior mentors, and corporate compliance officers.

**【Victor】**：Every single service downtime window had to be negotiated down to the minute. If our data integrity verification script failed during the midnight switchover, the rollback procedure had to execute within 15 minutes. Through structured weekly milestone reviews, transparent risk logging, and clear contingency protocols, our team delivered the migration with zero unscheduled downtime and 100% verified data integrity.

**【授課教師】**：Victor, that is a textbook example of managerial engineering. In enterprise IT, soft skills—negotiation, downtime management, stakeholder communication—are precisely what prevent multi-million-dollar outages. Very well articulated!

## 🏛️ 授課教師總評：專業術語精準度、肢體台風與即席問答心態

**【授課教師】**：Thank you so much to all the presenters today! You all demonstrated tremendous growth compared to Week 01. However, to elevate your performance to true executive standard, I want to emphasize three essential communication principles:

**【授課教師】**：First, **Language Precision & Technical Vocabulary**: In one of the Q&A exchanges earlier, when a student was asked how to protect the "integrity" of the platform, there was a momentary freeze trying to translate "integrity" into Chinese. Remember: in computer science and cybersecurity, **"Integrity" (完整性)** means ensuring that data and systems cannot be tampered with or modified by unauthorized parties. If you encounter a momentary language gap in an international meeting, never panic! Pause, paraphrase the concept in simple operational terms—such as "ensuring the data remains accurate, untampered, and reliable"—and maintain your composure.

**【授課教師】**：Second, **Body Language & Voice Projection**: When presenting your achievements, step out from behind the podium. Stand firmly, make genuine eye contact across the entire room, and use intentional pauses. When you state a major accomplishment—such as winning an international award or completing a 3-month zero-downtime migration—pause for two seconds. Let the magnitude of that achievement register with your audience.

**【授課教師】**：Third, **Audience-Centric Q&A Handling**: Always acknowledge the questioner with gratitude ("Thank you, that's a critical point"), restate the core question to ensure alignment, and frame your answer from the stakeholder's perspective. You all did a fantastic job today. For the remaining students who have not yet presented, we will continue our presentations next week. Thank you, everyone, and class dismissed!
