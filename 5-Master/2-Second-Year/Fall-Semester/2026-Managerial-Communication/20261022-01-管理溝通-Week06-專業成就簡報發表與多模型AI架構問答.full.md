## 🎤 Lucy 專業成就發表：多模型 AI 整合架構與跨領域溝通實踐

**【Lucy】**：Good morning, everyone. Today I'm pleased to share my professional accomplishments in AI engineering and technical communication. Throughout my graduate research and industry projects, I have focused on three core pillars: first, adopting cutting-edge technologies to solve unstructured business problems; second, ensuring AI system reliability by diving deep into messy real-world data; and third, communicating complex technical findings clearly to diverse stakeholders, including therapists, software engineers, and international business audiences.

**【Lucy】**：In my main project, I developed an intelligent prompt generator designed for enterprise workflows. The system automatically structures user intents and coordinates prompt execution across diverse domains. Why is this important? Because in modern enterprise settings, domain experts often know what they want to achieve, but they struggle with prompt engineering nuances. By bridging this gap, our tool reduced human trial-and-error by over 40% and significantly increased workflow efficiency.

**【Lucy】**：Furthermore, working with therapists and clinical specialists taught me that technology can never be treated in isolation. Communicating with non-technical experts requires complete translation of algorithmic parameters into human-centric language. This experience fundamentally shaped my engineering philosophy: building AI that is not only mathematically sophisticated, but genuinely explainable and trusted by practitioners. Thank you very much for listening!

## 💬 現場 Q&A 互動攻防：模型風險分散與跨領域資料整合挑戰

**【授課教師】**：Thank you so much, Lucy, for that concise and well-paced presentation. You kept your timing sharp and your slides visually clean. Before I provide detailed feedback, let's open the floor to the audience. We still have a few minutes for questions. Who would like to ask first? Yes, Vidi, please go ahead.

**【Vidi】**：Hi Lucy, thank you for your insightful presentation. I have a question regarding your prompt generator architecture: why did you decide to integrate three different AI models instead of relying on just one leading foundation model, such as GPT-4?

**【Lucy】**：That's an excellent question, Vidi. In real enterprise environments, depending exclusively on a single AI foundation model introduces severe vendor lock-in and operational risks. First, commercial API models frequently update their backends without prior notice, which can unpredictably alter output formatting and degrade prompt fidelity. Second, pricing and quota limitations differ significantly across providers.

**【Lucy】**：By orchestrating three distinct models—combining specialized open-source models for sensitive local reasoning with commercial APIs for open-ended synthesis—we achieve much higher resilience, lower latency, and better cost predictability. If one provider experiences downtime or API rate limits, our fallback mechanism seamlessly reroutes the request, ensuring zero disruption to client operations.

**【授課教師】**：Very sharp and convincing justification, Lucy. Acknowledging operational risk is a hallmark of managerial maturity. Let's take another question from the audience. Julie, please go ahead.

**【Julie】**：Hi Lucy, what was the most difficult bottleneck or obstacle you encountered when conducting your empirical research and model validation?

**【Lucy】**：Thank you, Julie. The hardest part was undeniably the data engineering and cross-source synchronization. Because our research required combining heterogeneous datasets from clinical observations, textual notes, and tabular interaction logs, the data formats were extremely noisy and inconsistent. I spent nearly two-thirds of the project timeline conducting exploratory data cleaning, outlier reconciliation, and building robust preprocessing pipelines before any model tuning could even begin.

**【授課教師】**：Exactly! In real-world data science and managerial analytics, 80% of the battle is fought in the trenches of data preprocessing and assumption validation. Lucy handled the question-and-answer session with strong composure, maintained direct eye contact with the questioners, and avoided defensive language. That is precisely what managerial communication demands. Let's give Lucy another round of applause!
